# About Me

## Who I Am

Hi, I'm **Udhaya Moorthy** — a Software Engineer and developer who enjoys building products that sit at the intersection of **technology, design, and real-world problems**.

I started my journey primarily through frontend development, spending a significant amount of time working with **React, TypeScript, modern UI architectures, and design systems**. Over time, I became increasingly interested in what happens beyond the interface — how applications are structured, how APIs and databases work together, how systems handle real-world complexity, and how AI can be integrated into useful products.

Today, I'm gradually expanding from frontend development toward **backend engineering, AI engineering, and broader software engineering concepts**.

I don't want to be limited to being someone who only builds what is visible on the screen. I want to understand the system behind it, the decisions that shape it, and how all the pieces work together.

---

# My Approach

I believe good software is more than code that simply works.

It should be:

**Useful** — solving an actual problem rather than existing only as a technical demonstration.

**Understandable** — structured in a way that another developer can reason about and maintain.

**Thoughtful** — considering the experience of the person who will actually use it.

**Scalable** — designed with enough awareness of future requirements without over-engineering everything from day one.

**Continuously improving** — because the first working implementation is rarely the final one.

This mindset influences how I approach both development and learning.

Whenever I build something, I try to understand not only *how* to implement it, but also *why* a particular approach makes sense.

# How I Learn

I'm naturally a **build-first learner**.

I understand concepts much better when I can connect them to something practical.

Instead of learning a technology purely through definitions, I prefer asking:

> Where would I actually use this?

> What problem does this solve?

> What happens if I don't use it?

> What are the trade-offs?

> How would this behave in a real application?

That's why many of my learning paths eventually turn into projects, experiments, prototypes, or small tools.

For me, learning isn't about collecting technologies.

It's about building a mental model of how software works.

# Working With AI

AI has also become part of the way I develop software.

I use AI tools to accelerate development, explore unfamiliar technologies, debug problems, generate starting points, and investigate different implementation approaches.

But I don't want AI to replace my understanding of the code.

My goal is to reach a point where I can:

```text
Understand the problem
        ↓
Design the approach
        ↓
Use AI to accelerate implementation
        ↓
Review the generated code
        ↓
Question the trade-offs
        ↓
Test and improve it
```

I see AI as a development multiplier rather than a substitute for engineering fundamentals.

That distinction has become increasingly important to me as I've started working with more complex systems.
